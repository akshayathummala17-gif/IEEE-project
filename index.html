The IEEE Technology and Engineering Management Society (TEMS) is the premier global professional society dedicated to the advancement of technology management, leadership, innovation, and entrepreneurship.

As a student chapter, our mission is to develop well-rounded technical professionals equipped to lead cross-functional teams, manage complex engineering initiatives, and translate breakthrough technologies into viable products and enterprises.

We bridge the traditional divide between technical specialization and organizational strategy. Through masterclasses, executive mentorship, leadership case studies, and hands-on governance, members gain the operational confidence required in high-stakes technological environments.

PILLAR 01

Dual Competency Development

Cultivate technical depth alongside strategic business acumen, preparing you for roles such as Technical Product Manager, Engineering Lead, and Tech Founder.

PILLAR 02

Global Body of Knowledge (TEMSBOK)

Learn from IEEE TEMS standard practices in technology roadmapping, project governance, innovation ecosystems, and risk assessment.

PILLAR 03

Direct Industry Executive Access

Engage directly with CTOs, project directors, and engineering heads through structured roundtable discussions and mentoring pods.

While primarily designed for students in engineering, computer science, and technology disciplines seeking management and leadership competence, we also warmly welcome business and design students interested in leading tech-focused initiatives.
No prior business or management experience is required. The chapter exists specifically to teach engineering students these competencies through structured, practical modules, real case studies, and mentorship from industry practitioners.
Traditional IEEE chapters (such as IEEE Computer Society, Signal Processing, or Power & Energy) concentrate exclusively on specific technical disciplines. IEEE TEMS is unique because it focuses on the essential complementary discipline: how to manage technology, lead multidisciplinary teams, govern budgets, and take innovation to market.
General active members invest approximately 2 to 4 hours per month attending workshops, keynote sessions, and networking events. Student committee leads and project officers typically dedicate 4 to 6 hours per week managing initiatives and receiving direct mentorship.
